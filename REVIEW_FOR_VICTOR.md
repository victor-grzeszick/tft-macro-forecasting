### M1 · ARIMA is fitted on train+validation and then scored on validation (KI-1, CONFIRMED)
- **Evidence.** `src/baseline.py:79` builds `df = concat(df_train, df_val)` and `:93` passes it as `train_series` to `arima_rolling_forecast`. `:27` runs `auto_arima` on 1990-01–2019-12. `:29` then rolls over `concat(train+val, val)`, so 2018–2019 appears twice. The forecast labelled 2018-03 (h=3) comes from a model whose orders and parameters have already seen all of 2018–2019. The `.update()` calls then re-estimate on the duplicated months.
- **Why it matters.** All six ARIMA rows in `Baseline_Results.csv` have look-ahead. The ARIMA orders in your notes, (0,0,3), (5,0,1) and (1,0,3), are the orders from this train+val fit; the audit reproduced them exactly. `spec.md:32` says "on the training set". ARIMA has the lowest baseline RMSE in 4 of 6 cells (CPI h3/h12, UNRATE h12, INDPRO h3). `dm_test.py` uses that choice, so ARIMA is the comparator in the CPI and INDPRO h=3 DM tests, and every TFT-vs-ARIMA comparison on validation is affected.
- **What it would change.** The ARIMA validation RMSE/MAE values, possibly the orders, possibly which baseline is "best" per cell, and the three DM statistics.
- **Options.**
  - (a) Fit `auto_arima` on train only, then roll through validation with `.update()`. Clean validation numbers, and orders chosen on 1990–2017 as the spec says. Every ARIMA number and possibly the orders change; notes and figures need updating.
  - (b) Keep the code and state the contamination wherever ARIMA validation numbers appear. No re-run, but the "best baseline" choice stays biased toward ARIMA.
  - (c) Keep the code for the test stage only. Fitting on train+val and rolling through 2020–2024 is the standard design, because the bug only bites when the evaluation window is inside the fitting window. Validation ARIMA would still need (a) or (b).

### M3 · Checkpoints hold final-epoch weights, not best-validation weights (new)
- **Evidence.** `src/trainer.py:18-22`: `EarlyStopping(monitor="val_loss", patience=15)`, then `trainer.fit`, then `trainer.save_checkpoint`. There is no `ModelCheckpoint` and no restore of the best weights. Every final checkpoint records `wait_count = 15`, so the saved weights are from 15 epochs after the best `val_loss`.
- **Why it matters.** The TFT you evaluate is not the early-stopped model the notes describe. It may be slightly worse (or better) on validation than the best epoch.

### M7 · DM test: statistic only, h=3 only, and the "artifact" explanation (new)
- **Evidence.** `src/dm_test.py:53-57` computes d̄ / √(HAC variance / n). There is no p-value, no stars (`spec.md:43` asks for them) and no small-sample correction. h=12 is dropped (`:36`). Stored values (validation, 2018-03–2019-12, n=22): CPI ARIMA vs TFT −2.139, UNRATE VAR vs TFT +0.592, INDPRO ARIMA vs TFT −2.152.
- **The explanation.** `notes_models.md:91` calls these "artifacts" of a near-zero variance. The DM statistic is scale-invariant: multiplying all errors by c scales both d̄ and √variance by c². The baseline-vs-baseline tests you treat as informative had variances of the same order (5.3e-13 to 1.4e-3, recomputed from the committed predictions). Low power (n=22), no small-sample correction, and 9+3 tests on the same window are real caveats; "division by ~0" may not be.
- **Options.**
  - (a) Add p-values (normal or Harvey-Leybourne-Newbold small-sample t) and keep the caveats.
  - (b) Report statistics only and drop the "artifact" wording until you have re-derived it.
  - Either way, h=12 DM would first appear on the test set.
